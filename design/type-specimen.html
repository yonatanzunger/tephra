Nothing moves. The rail and the sheet are fixed columns, and the annotation gutter is reserved in every arrangement — so marginal notes can arrive later without reflowing a single line.

Faces ship with macOS. Hoefler Text, Iowan Old Style, Charter and New York. Nothing is downloaded, so what you see is what the app can use without bundling a font.

The margin is for commentary. Its notes are sized and toned to be read, not glanced at — the tradition this layout borrows from treats the margin as a place where the argument continues.

Hebrew is sized up. Its letters all sit at x-height and several differ only in fine detail, so it needs more pixels than Latin at the same nominal size. Done with a unicode-range and size-adjust, so no markup or language tagging is involved — and the face is chosen, not inherited, since naming one replaces the natural fallback.

What the editing spike actually settled

The measurement exists so that nobody has to talk themselves into a bad number. Typing at a hundred and thirty words a minute, the editor contributes well under a millisecond of work per keystroke, and everything else in the figure is the browser's input pipeline and the display refresh. That is a more useful result than the one we set out to get.

Worth re-running against a 25 MB corpus before the extent cap is ever raised.

The bar we wrote down — p99 under thirty milliseconds — turned out not to be answerable as stated. The same measurement over a plain text area holding the identical megabyte comes back at 17.5 ms, so most of that budget belongs to the compositor rather than to us. What is unambiguous is the part the bar was standing in for.
